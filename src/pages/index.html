Show what you shipped.

Showship reads your commits, pull requests, code reviews and tasks, then builds the progress deck your manager, client or CTO can follow. Weekly, per sprint, monthly or for any date range.

npx showship
Read the documentation

An agent skill for Claude Code, plus Codex and Antigravity (experimental). Needs Node.js 18 or newer, git and the GitHub CLI. Beta release. Download the example deck ({{size:assets/downloads/example-deck-showship.pptx}}).

What the deck covers

One deck answers the six questions every audience asks.

Business readers get plain language, with technical terms only in brackets when they add something. Engineers get the technical version. Either way, every item says what changed for users or for the business.

  1. What are we working on?

    Projects and features, one card each on the executive summary.

    Slide: dashboard

  2. What has been delivered?

    Done, released, approved and merged, in the words the business uses.

    Slides: dashboard, timeline, workstreams

  3. How far along is it?

    Percent and phases for each track. A footnote says how the percentage was counted.

    Slides: rings, phase_bars

  4. Who is doing what?

    Owners per track, and a task track per person from your task tracker.

    Slide: tracker

  5. What is blocked, and what do you need from us?

    Blockers and risks, plus the decisions you need, each with an owner or its options.

    Slide: attention

  6. What happens next?

    Next week, sprint or month: owner, focus and status.

    Slide: table

How it works

Collect in each repo. Build once.

The set of repos changes from period to period, so the work is split in two. Run collect inside every repo that had work, then run build once from anywhere.

  1. Collect

    Reads commits on all branches and every pull request with activity: descriptions, reviews and comments in order. Bots are filtered out.

    Writes a plain-language draft per repo.

  2. Add context

    Pulls tasks from your tracker, and asks one question for what GitHub cannot tell it: a percentage, a blocker outside the code.

    Your answers go into notes.md.

  3. Build

    Merges the drafts and notes into report.json, runs the plain-language check, and writes the .pptx.

    Edits are changes to the JSON, then a rebuild.

  4. Check

    Renders every slide and flags text that crosses the margins or collides with other text.

    Needs LibreOffice. Without it, the agent says the deck was not rendered.

You talk, the agent works.

Ask in your own language. The deck is written in the language you configure. Showship never invents numbers, owners or dates: they come from your data or from you.

text
# run inside each repo that had work this week

# once, after the last repo

Slide layouts

Twelve layouts, picked to fit your data.

Consecutive slides never reuse a layout, charts appear where there are real numbers, and every layout renders in all six designs.

dashboard
Executive summary: one card per project, plus a key update.
timeline
Milestones, or a blocker that was resolved.
rings
Doughnut charts for overall progress, with status tiles.
phase_bars
Phase-by-phase completion per track, with owners.
workstreams
A finished item beside a process flow.
rows
Icon rows with a generated illustration or a screenshot.
gallery
A strip of UI screenshots with step labels.
activity
Team delivery activity from GitHub, as a chart.
review_insights
What code review verified and caught before release.
attention
Blockers and risks, and the decisions needed from the business.
tracker
Developer task track, from your task tracker and reviews.
table
Next period's plan with owners and status.

Who it is for

Team leads reporting up. Individuals reporting their own work.

Two settings, role and audience, decide whose work is collected and how technical the deck may be. The installer asks for them, and you can change them in config.json at any time.

You areSetYou get
Team lead reporting to business or managementrole: lead
audience: business		The whole team's progress in plain language: owners, percentages, blockers, decisions needed, what is next.
Team lead reporting to a CTO or engineering orgrole: lead
audience: engineering		The same, with technical detail kept: architecture, test status, review findings.
Developer reporting to a tech lead or managerrole: member
audience: engineering		Only your commits, the PRs you wrote or reviewed, and your tasks.
Freelancer or contractor reporting to a clientrole: member
audience: business		A client-ready update of your work, in their language, with your logo.
Solo founder updating investors or a co-founderrole: member
audience: business		A short progress deck built from your repos.

Tasks come from the tracker you already use.

Jira Cloud, Linear, Asana, ClickUp and Lark are built in. Any other tool works through an MCP server, read-only.

Your data stays on your machine.

There is no Showship server and no account. Commits, PRs and tasks are read locally, and tracker tokens stay in environment variables.

Install

Run one command, then answer a few questions.

npx showship

The installer copies the skill into your agent's skills folder, records your role, audience, reporting rhythm, task tracker and deck design in config.json, installs the deck builder, and checks for git, gh and LibreOffice.

  • Node.js 18 or newer, and git
  • The GitHub CLI, signed in with gh auth login
  • LibreOffice, optional: lets the agent render slides and check the layout

Showship is in beta. Slide templates, config keys and installer options may change between releases, and the Codex and Antigravity installs have seen less testing.
